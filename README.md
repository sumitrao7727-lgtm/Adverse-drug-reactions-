13. Professional ADR Narrative
A 52-year-old female weighing 68 kg with osteoarthritis of the knee was prescribed diclofenac 50 mg orally twice daily for pain management. Approximately five days after initiation of therapy, she developed epigastric pain, heartburn, nausea, and black-colored stools. Laboratory investigations revealed a hemoglobin level of 9.8 g/dL, and stool occult blood testing was positive. Upper gastrointestinal endoscopy revealed gastric mucosal ulceration with evidence of recent bleeding.

Diclofenac was discontinued, and the patient was treated with pantoprazole and supportive measures. Her symptoms improved following withdrawal of diclofenac. Based on the temporal association, known adverse-effect profile, objective evidence, absence of a significant alternative cause, and positive dechallenge, the reaction was assessed as probable/likely according to the WHO-UMC criteria. The Naranjo score was 7, indicating a probable adverse drug reaction.
